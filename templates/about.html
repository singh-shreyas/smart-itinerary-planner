{{ _('The problem') }}

{{ _("A traveller has a few days and far more attractions than time. Each attraction has a value (rating, popularity and whether it matches the traveller's interests), a visit duration, an entry fee and opening hours that differ by weekday. Moving between places takes time, and lunch and dinner have to fit in too. We must choose which attractions to visit on which day, and in what order, so the plan is worth as much as possible without breaking the daily time window, any opening hours or the meal windows. This is a travel-optimisation system: it optimises time and travel, not money.") }}

{{ _('This is the multi-day Orienteering Problem with Time Windows, which is NP-hard. So we do not claim optimal itineraries. We decompose it into sub-problems that each have a well-understood algorithm: shortest paths, selection, route ordering and scheduling.') }}

{{ _('Scoring and the knapsack model') }}

{{ _('Where is travel time in the knapsack?') }} {{ _("Travel depends on the order, which is decided after selection. So each stop's weight is visit + pace buffer + cheapest trip out of it, and the capacity is the day length minus the shortest first ride, the unavoidable extra of the ride home (beyond the last stop's cheapest exit) and the meal breaks. Every route leaves each stop exactly once, so these weights can only under-estimate the real day: the knapsack never throws away a set that truly fits. The scheduler then checks the real timeline, and repair fixes optimism.") }}

{{ _('Discretisation.') }} {{ _('Weights are rounded up to {n}-minute units and the capacity down. Every strategy receives the identical integer instance.', n=cfg.DP_TIME_UNIT_MIN) }}

{{ _('What "optimal" means here.') }} {{ _('DP is exact for this rounded knapsack model on each day. 2-opt reaches a local optimum. Held–Karp is exact for travel only, ignores opening hours, and is used on the comparison page as a lower bound. None of this makes the whole multi-day itinerary provably optimal.') }}

{{ _('Access: treks, ferries and walks') }}

{{ _("Not every sight is at the end of a road. A trek, a ferry crossing or a walk is an edge of the same graph as the roads, so Dijkstra finds mixed routes, such as the road to Gaurikund followed by the trek, and every leg lists its parts with their own times. The trek up and the trek down are separate directed edges, so each is counted once: in the knapsack weight, in routing, in the timeline and in the day's totals. The traffic factor applies to road parts only. A ferry with a known first and last departure makes you wait for the first one, and rules the trip out after the last one.") }}

{{ _("Times come only from sources: a duration a source states (Kedarnath trek “6 to 8 hours”: we use 8), or Naismith's rule applied to a sourced distance and climb (5 km/h plus 1 hour per 600 m ascent), labelled as an estimate. Helicopters, ropeways and battery cars are listed as other ways in, but never used in a plan, because their schedules and availability are not verified. A place whose access time is unknown is never given a guessed time. It is listed with its reason and a note to verify locally.") }}

{{ _('Where you stay') }}

{{ _("Every city offers five stay areas: North, South, East, West and Central. Each one is a real neighbourhood, suburb or town from OpenStreetMap, never a sight, a railway station or an airport, and preferably one with many places to stay nearby (counted from OpenStreetMap's hotels and guest houses). The direction is the compass sector seen from the city centre. Where the geography makes a direction meaningless, for example where the sea or a mountain valley lies that way, the closest area by direction is used and marked with * and a note that says where it really is. Stations remain in the data as transport points, kept apart from places to stay.") }}

{{ _("The stay area is a node of the travel graph with its own road links (OSRM), so every first ride, ride home, route order and travel score is computed from it. Changing the area changes the travel times; the cached tables are keyed to the database file, so they are rebuilt when the data changes. A stay area is a neighbourhood's map point, not a specific hotel.") }}

{{ _('Add up to {n} cities to your base city. Rule: every pair of cities must be within {km} km of each other in a straight line between their centres (not just each within {km} km of the base). {{ _('There are two ways to travel. With a moving stay (the default), you choose a starting stay and an ending stay, and the journey visits the cities in the order with the least road time between their stays. It finishes one city before moving on and never comes back to it (a round trip returns only at the end). The drive to the next stay is part of that travel day, so it uses real time in the timeline, and every night you sleep where the day ended. With one base, you stay in the same area every night and make day trips; each day is then planned around one city or several, whichever gives the best result, so the plan does not zig-zag between cities.') }}

{{ _('Limitations') }}

  • {{ _('The full problem is NP-hard; select-then-route is a decomposition heuristic and can miss a better combination (DP can lose to Greedy on a whole trip).') }}
  • {{ _('When the planner chooses the places, it does so day by day in date order (myopic); the itinerary mode then re-assigns that set to days, but does not swap in places the selection left out.') }}
  • {{ _('Assigning places to days is NP-hard; the modes use a heuristic search, checked against exhaustive search only on small cases.') }}
  • {{ _('Most hours, fees and visit times in the newer cities are category defaults; closing days are unknown.') }}
  • {{ _('Multi-city eligibility uses straight-line distance, and a journey stays in the central stay area of each city it passes through (only the first and last stays are chosen by you).') }}
  • {{ _('Trek and walking times are estimates from cited distances and climbs; ferry timetables and weather closures are not live. Places with unknown access times are not scheduled.') }}
  • {{ _('Overnight stops on a trek (for example at Kedarnath) are not modelled: every day ends at a stay area.') }}
  • {{ _('Stay areas are OpenStreetMap neighbourhood points; the “places to stay nearby” count depends on OSM coverage, which is thin in small towns.') }}
  • {{ _('Food and shopping coverage depends on Wikipedia and is thin in small towns.') }}
  • {{ _('Map lines are straight segments in visit order, not road geometry.') }}
